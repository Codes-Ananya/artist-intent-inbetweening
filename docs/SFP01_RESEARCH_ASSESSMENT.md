# SFP-01 research assessment — accepted exploratory demonstration

The present result is defensible as a transparent engineering case study and failure-analysis artifact. It does **not yet establish a novel interpolation method, generalizable quality improvement, or artist-intent preservation**. This judgment concerns the project's evidence, not a completed literature/novelty review.

| Experiment | Supported observation | Claim not supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three-input baseline (0,4,7) | Local RIFE/CUDA execution, exact supplied PNG preservation and exports work. The small settle is more coherent than cross-dissolve; the larger reach fades/smears. | Overall quality superiority, independent motion accuracy or production readiness. |
| Extra breakdown (0,2,4,7) | Supplied frame 2 constrains the timeline; newly generated frame 3 is cleaner, but frame 1 still fails. | Equal-information algorithm advantage: it uses four inputs versus three. Supplied frame 2 is not a generated success. |

| Original/refined layers | Explicit opaque foreground placement and observed-pixel patches reduce the visible fade/smear; a single manual refinement improves the seam and arm size. | Automatic motion recovery: masks, joints, angles, ROI and reconstruction introduce extra manual information. Greater opacity is partly guaranteed by construction. |

The user accepted the refined frame **as an exploratory demonstration**, with manual interventions and residual elbow/transition defects disclosed. Acceptance is neither an independent accuracy label nor an artist-intent study. The accepted result still uses 255 inpaint-derived pixels under the recorded masks, 47 not fully covered by the new arm. Exact preserved endpoints are a useful implementation guarantee, but do not guarantee correct intermediate motion. CPU/GPU timings omit differing amounts of manual setup and are not comparable workflow-efficiency measurements.

## Potential contribution

A plausible direction is a reproducible, effort-aware study of when explicit region/occlusion guidance helps stylized interpolation and when it fails. The contribution would be the evaluated workflow, failure taxonomy and evidence—not the existing RIFE backend, basic affine compositing or an untested novelty claim. Current coordinate-specific masks/angles are tailored to one image; there is no reusable guidance interface or held-out evaluation. Before making a publication claim, establish what is distinct from prior guided interpolation/compositing work through a scoped literature review and an appropriately controlled study. A single synthetic success after manual edits is presently insufficient for a publication-ready method claim.

## Smallest useful next experiment: a frozen transfer pilot

One new transition is the absolute minimum transfer check, but is easily misleading. A small useful pilot is **three preregistered, independently sourced transitions**: two arm-over-torso occlusion cases with different pose/appearance (at least one different character), plus one small-motion or low-occlusion control. Keep Track A/D unchanged; use a separately authorized exploratory roster. Select existing licensed/consented sequences with withheld intermediate frames where practical, rather than generating or selecting cases after seeing outputs. None is selected or generated in this task.

Before evaluation, externalize masks, joints and transforms into a per-case annotation configuration and freeze the compositing procedure. Do not transfer SFP coordinates as if they generalized. An annotator may see only endpoint images, not withheld intermediate references or the baseline outputs while choosing guidance. Predeclare the allowed information: endpoint arm masks, shoulder/elbow locations, placement rule, visible-source patches, and the explicit unknown-region policy. Target placement remains manual guidance and must be charged as such. No iterative output-driven retuning. If a case requires extra artwork beyond the allowance, report failure/abstention and the missing information.

Compare archived/default RIFE scale 1 with one guided layered candidate per case, identical image endpoints/timing/resolution. Set a small annotation/edit budget before starting (for example 20 minutes per case, explicitly an initial protocol choice, not a measured feasibility promise); log actual time, masks, patch area, reconstructed area and edit count. Add a budget-matched direct manual correction if claiming practical effort savings. Extra-breakdown conditions are optional later and must disclose their extra supplied poses; avoid expanding this first transfer pilot into a parameter sweep.

Primary exploratory outcome: whether occlusion/contour defects improve **without worsening anatomy or temporal transitions**, assessed on full clips with randomized condition order and separate ratings of opacity, contours, joint shape and transition continuity. Seek at least two independent reviewers and retain disagreements. References, if available, stay hidden during annotation; use them for reference-based comparisons, with foreground-region measures alongside full-frame measures to avoid background dominance. Do not reduce success to opacity or pixel similarity alone. Report all three cases, including failures and effort. This can reveal transfer failure or justify a larger study; three cases cannot establish broad generalization or statistical superiority.

Decision: stop tuning SFP-01. Retain it as an accepted, explicitly manual demonstration and a documented negative/positive diagnostic chain. Prioritize the small transfer pilot only after its inputs, annotation budget and review protocol are authorized; no new poses or experiments are initiated here.
